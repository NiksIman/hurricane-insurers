Hurricanes & Insurers

Does the “Pricing Poseidon” result hold out of sample in 2020–2024? An event study of 16 US hurricane landfalls, focused on Florida-concentrated P&C insurers, with 7 earlier storms for comparison.

What it means

The sell-off before landfall is a stable pattern. The four Florida insurers lose clearly against the market in the week before, even for storms that end up making landfall elsewhere. Forecast cones often cross Florida.

Whether a recovery follows depends on the actual damage compared with what was feared. Irma in 2017 and Milton in 2024 turned away from the feared target area, and prices quickly made up the drop. Ian in 2022 hit the core of the Florida books: loss estimates kept rising, one carrier went into receivership, and the stocks kept falling.

The size of the pre-landfall drop does not predict the rebound. Each 1% of drop came back by –% within 5 days on average, but the 95% interval – includes zero.

Uncertainty stays elevated after landfall, for weeks and into the second and third month. That matches the paper. Price data cannot tell whether options price this uncertainty too cheaply or too richly.

Method

  • Prices: adjusted daily closes from Yahoo Finance, 2004 to June 2025. ACIC carries the UIHC history, EG the RE history.
  • Abnormal returns: market model estimated on trading days −250 to −30 before T0, once against SPY and once against KIE. Stocks with more than 30% zero-return days in the estimation window are excluded. This only affects the pre-2020 comparison: UVE for Katrina 2005 (then a penny stock) and ACIC, then UIHC, for Ike, Irene and Sandy.
  • Timing: T0 is the first close after landfall. For six storms, landfall came after the close or on a weekend. A check run with the brief's dates barely changes the result.
  • Independence: storms less than 20 trading days apart form one cluster. The 16 events in 2020–2024 make only 7 clusters. Standard errors and bootstrap intervals run over these clusters.

Limits

  • Six Florida landfalls in five clusters is very little. Read the results as case studies, not as hard statistics.
  • Other drivers overlap the storms: the Florida reform of 2022/23, the hard reinsurance market, earnings (HRTG after Debby) and market shocks (Debby fell on 5 August 2024, Ike on the Lehman day).
  • The left tail is partly missing. UPC sits inside ACIC; FedNat is missing entirely and only enters as a scenario.
  • Before 2020 the FL stocks only exist from 2007/08 (HRTG from 2014), so the comparison is thin.
  • The observation in the brief that insurers beat the market in the month after landfall does not hold here. National insurers and reinsurers end near zero after 20 days.

Next steps

  • Tier 2: get prices for historical implied volatility (ORATS, Cboe DataShop, OptionMetrics). First check whether UVE, HCI, HRTG and ACIC have enough option trading at all.
  • Forecast vs outcome: compare the NHC forecast cone on the day before landfall with the actual landfall location. It is the one candidate that could separate rebounds like Milton from cases like Ian in advance.
